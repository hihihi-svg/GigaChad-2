Mission 4 – Task 2: Full Dataset Streaming and Loss Accounting

All 144 audio clips from Team 11 were concatenated in their original order and streamed to the ESP32-S3 at real-time speed (1.0×) using 16 kHz sampling, int16 encoding, 320 samples per packet, and 40 log-mel features.

The input contained 2,304,000 samples, corresponding to 144 seconds of audio and 4,608,000 bytes. The expected packet count was 7,200, and the board successfully accepted all 7,200 data packets.

The continuous stream produced 7,199 feature frames. All 7,199 frames were received, with zero lost feature frames, zero sequence gaps, and zero CRC errors. The ESP32 stored the complete 4,608,000-byte audio payload in PSRAM without overflow.

The average feature computation time was 1,622 µs, and the maximum was 1,805 µs.

Conclusion: The ESP32-S3 successfully processed and stored the complete 144-clip dataset at real-time speed without packet loss or feature-frame loss.
